Keeping Your New York City HVAC System Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a system that lasts and one that quits in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your equipment plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

Minor issues rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a miserable night.

Comfort Challenges New York City Homeowners Face

Most local HVAC failures announce themselves before they happen. A breaker that trips every few days, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, a musty smell at startup. These are the early symptoms of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're a quick visit. Left alone, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
