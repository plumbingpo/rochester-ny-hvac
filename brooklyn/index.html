When Your System Fails in Brooklyn

Call us with a problem and a real person picks up, not a recording. Most service requests get scheduled within a day, and urgent calls, an AC that quits in a heat wave or a furnace that goes out on a bitter night, go straight to the front of the line. Scheduling stays flexible so nobody is left waiting for a callback.

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: an air conditioner blowing warm air, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that ignites and shuts off, a breaker that keeps tripping, an evaporator coil that has frozen, a condensate drain backed up into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has gone blank. Most of them trace back to parts already on our trucks: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and common fittings. You get the price before any work starts, with no surprises on the invoice.

Two situations call for a quick step from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or produces a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. That small step can save you from a far bigger repair.

Keeping Your Brooklyn HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Small problems don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
